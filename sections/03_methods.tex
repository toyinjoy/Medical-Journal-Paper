\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

This section defines the four outcome tasks, leakage-controlled evaluation regimes, generators, selection rule, factorial mechanism analysis, common benchmark, controlled overlap experiment, and reproducibility safeguards.

\subsection{Datasets Definition and Description}
\label{sec:datasets}

\subsubsection{Vigitel health survey}

Vigitel is Brazil's telephone-based surveillance system for risk and protective factors for chronic non-communicable diseases \citep{bernal2017}. The obesity experiments use a reproducible stratified sample of 5,000 rows drawn from a 2006--2023 extract, comprising 4,149 non-obese and 851 obese records (imbalance ratio approximately 4.9:1). The factorial experiment uses 15 predictors: age, sex, current and former smoking, physical inactivity, leisure-time physical activity, diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, dietary indicators, fruit and vegetable regularity, and schooling. Not every variable in the source extract is used; identifiers, the outcome, and variables excluded by the prespecified analytic pipeline are omitted.

\subsubsection{COVID-19 hospital mortality}

The COVID-19 dataset contains 334 hospital records and 34 columns, including the binary target \texttt{obito} (death). The executed experiments report 251 survivors and 83 deaths, an imbalance ratio of approximately 3.0:1. Predictors include age, sex, alcohol and tobacco indicators, admission-reason groups, number of comorbidities, and specific conditions such as hypertension, heart failure, coronary disease, chronic respiratory disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, cancer, cirrhosis, and HIV.

The small sample size and the clinical heterogeneity of the death class are scientifically important rather than incidental. A synthetic-selection method can appear to improve separability simply by concentrating on an artificially homogeneous subset of deaths; results on this dataset must therefore be interpreted with wide uncertainty and assessed strictly on held-out real folds.

\subsubsection{Vigitel current smoking}

The third task uses the supplied 21,764-record Vigitel analytic sample with current smoking (\texttt{fumante}) as the outcome. The executed experiment uses a reproducible stratified sample of 5,000 records: 616 current smokers and 4,384 nonsmokers. Identifiers, colour labels, and upstream cluster fields are excluded; 27 demographic, socioeconomic, diet, activity, and chronic-condition variables are retained. Because the supplied file has no survey-year or sampling-weight field, results describe the analytic sample rather than a weighted population parameter.

\subsubsection{Kidney-cohort 365-day mortality}

The fourth task is derived from 9,559,286 dated events for 67,267 patients. Each patient's landmark is 365 days after first recorded activity, and 15 predictor counts are computed strictly before that date. The outcome is recorded death during the subsequent 365 days. Patients who died before the landmark are excluded; nondeaths require observable activity through the horizon. The eligible cohort has a 5.34\% event rate; the executed stratified sample contains 5,000 patients, 267 deaths and 4,733 nondeaths. Dialysis is not an outcome in this study.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Cross-validation Protocol}
\label{sec:preprocessing}

All predictive experiments use stratified five-fold cross-validation. Binary variables are normalized to $\{0,1\}$, and 1/2-coded survey fields are mapped deterministically. Within each fold, numerical missing values are median-imputed, binary fields use the modal category, and continuous variables are standardized to zero mean and unit variance using statistics fitted only on that fold's real training subset. CTGAN fitting, candidate scoring, selection, and classifier training all occur within the training portion; the held-out real fold is used only for evaluation.

Every predictive result reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} follows this fold-contained protocol. Within each fold of the factorial experiment, a single CTGAN model generates a candidate pool five times the real training size, and the same pool is scored under all four selection conditions described in Section~\ref{sec:factorial}. Pairing removes generator-to-generator variation between conditions, but the five fold scores are not independent replications: training folds overlap by 75\%, and the four conditions within a fold share a candidate pool. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses this dependence, and the primary factorial model blocks on fold.

We distinguish exploratory from confirmatory evidence throughout. All numerical claims in Sections~\ref{sec:results} and \ref{sec:discussion} come from executed analyses and exported result tables; protocol-only, superseded single-split, or failed runs are not reported as evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}). Results from different evaluation regimes are not pooled because standalone synthetic substitution, real-plus-synthetic augmentation, and internal synthetic cross-validation answer different questions.

\subsection{Evaluation Regimes}
\label{sec:regimes}

Three regimes are kept distinct throughout:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.4cm,style=nextline]
\item[\ra{} (TRTR):] \textit{train on four real folds and evaluate on the fifth real fold, this is the predictive reference.}
\item[\sr{} (TSTR):] \textit{train only on synthetic records generated from a real training fold and test on the corresponding held-out real fold, this measures substitution utility.}
\item[\rsr{}:] \textit{append synthetic positive-class records to the real training fold and test on held-out real data; this measures augmentation, not synthetic substitution.}
\end{description}

Macro-F1 is the endpoint of the original mechanism experiments because both outcome classes contribute equally. In the common augmentation analysis, AUPRC is the prespecified primary endpoint. Secondary outcomes are Macro-F1, positive-class F1, precision and recall, AUROC, balanced accuracy, Brier score, and calibration intercept and slope. Metrics not produced by the original executed pipeline are reported as unavailable rather than retrospectively reconstructed.

\subsection{Generators and Predictive Classifiers}
\label{sec:generators}

CTGAN \citep{xu2019} is the generator in the mechanism experiments. The common augmentation analysis adds TVAE \citep{xu2019} to test whether the selection mechanism transfers beyond a GAN. Standard-generator and \sepa{} conditions share a candidate pool within a fold. Because Synthetic Data Vault (SDV) version 1.17 does not implement native conditional sampling for TVAE, TVAE is fitted directly to positive-class training-fold feature records and the outcome label is assigned by design; this is reported separately from CTGAN's outcome-conditional sampling. Predictive models in the common analysis are logistic regression, random forest, and CatBoost \citep{prokhorenkova2018}; the mechanism experiments also use XGBoost \citep{chen2016} and TabPFN \citep{hollmann2025}.

\subsection{\sepa{} Selection Framework}
\label{sec:sepaware}

Within one fold, let $\mathcal D_R=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ denote the real training data and $\mathcal C=\{(\tilde x_j,\tilde y_j)\}_{j=1}^{m}$ an oversized synthetic candidate pool. Numerical fields are median-imputed, standardized using the training-fold mean and standard deviation, and concatenated with one-hot encodings of categorical fields. Denote this fitted mixed-type representation by $\phi(x)$. All quantities below are fitted from $\mathcal D_R$ and evaluated on $\mathcal C$; the held-out fold is not used.

For candidate $j$, the nearest-real distance is
\begin{equation}
d_j=\min_i\|\phi(\tilde x_j)-\phi(x_i)\|_2.
\end{equation}
For a vector $z$, robust normalization uses $c=\operatorname{median}(z)$ and $s=1.4826\operatorname{median}|z-c|$. If $s\le10^{-12}$, $s=\operatorname{IQR}(z)/1.349$; if that value is also at most $10^{-12}$, every normalized value is set to $0.5$. Otherwise,
\begin{equation}
N(z_j)=\frac{\operatorname{clip}((z_j-c)/s,-3,3)+3}{6}.
\end{equation}
The realism score is $R_j=1-N(d_j)$ and lies in $[0,1]$.

Let $h_j$ be the distance to the nearest real record with label $\tilde y_j$, and let $q_j$ be the distance to the nearest real record with the opposite label. The geometric margin is $g_j=q_j-h_j$. A random forest fitted only to $\mathcal D_R$ gives assigned-label probability $p_j=\widehat P(Y=\tilde y_j\mid\tilde x_j)$. The separability score is
\begin{equation}
Sep_j=\tfrac12N(g_j)+\tfrac12N(p_j),
\end{equation}
which also lies in $[0,1]$. When independently prespecified anchors exist, $A_j\in[0,1]$ is the mean of their class-compatible indicators; otherwise $A_j=0$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}=0$. The composite score is
\begin{equation}
S_j=R_j+\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}Sep_j+\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}A_j,
\label{eq:sepaware}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}=1$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}=0$ in the common benchmark. Within each class, candidates are ordered by decreasing $S_j$ and then by their original candidate index, which provides deterministic tie-breaking. The first $Q_y$ candidates are retained for class $y$. If fewer than $Q_y$ candidates exist, execution stops with a shortfall error rather than duplicating records. Exact nearest-neighbour scoring requires $O(mn)$ distance evaluations and $O(m\log m)$ sorting, apart from classifier fitting.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Fit imputation, numerical scaling, and categorical encoding on $\mathcal D_R$.
\item If necessary, generate a candidate pool larger than the requested class totals using the recorded seed.
\item Fit the scoring random forest on the represented real training data.
\item For every candidate, compute nearest-real distance and $R_j$.

\item If prespecified anchors apply, calculate and normalize $A_j$; otherwise set its weight to zero.
\item Form $S_j$; rank within class by $(-S_j,j)$; select the first $Q_y$ records or raise a shortfall error.

The earlier fixed-$\lambda$ grid used $\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}\in\{0,0.25,0.5\}$. Because configurations were compared on the reporting folds, that grid is exploratory and does not determine the common-benchmark setting. The dataset-specific factorial experiment used predeclared levels $\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}\in\{0,1\}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}\in\{0,0.5\}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:sepaware_pipeline} summarizes the pipeline. CTGAN produces candidates but does not itself optimize the three selection criteria; a separate selector scores and ranks candidates within each class and returns the requested class counts. Separating generation from selection makes the intervention auditable and allows component-wise ablation, which is the basis of the factorial design in Section~\ref{sec:factorial}. The design rules out one simple alternative explanation -- that a \tstr{} change is caused by a different class ratio -- because class counts are held fixed across all compared conditions. It does not rule out harder alternatives: ranking can select unusually easy prototypes, discard boundary cases, or amplify a classifier-specific shortcut. These possibilities motivate the confirmatory analyses proposed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Fidelity, Separability, and Privacy Proxies}
\label{sec:proxies}

Fidelity combines numerical-distribution similarity, categorical-proportion similarity, and correlation preservation into a single composite score. Separability is reported using cosine silhouette, a one-nearest-neighbor label-agreement index (SI), and hypothesis margin (HM). These are descriptive geometric measures: higher separation is not automatically clinically desirable, and we do not treat it as such (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

The common benchmark measures held-out-real positive-class coverage as the mean distance from each positive test record to its nearest synthetic positive record after training-fold standardization. Positive-class diversity is the mean pairwise standardized distance among up to 1000 selected positive records. Exact overlap is the proportion of synthetic records with zero distance to a real training record. The membership-inference proxy compares each real training and held-out record's distance to the nearest synthetic record and reports membership-classification AUROC. These are empirical diagnostics, not evidence of anonymity or a formal guarantee.

\subsection{Domain-anchored Causal Plausibility Filtering}
\label{sec:dacpf}

\dacpf{} (Domain-Anchored Causal Plausibility Filtering) generates positive-class CTGAN candidates and ranks their consistency with real positive-class distributions for domain anchors. The completed COVID-19 experiment compares no augmentation, standard CTGAN augmentation, age filtering, disease-anchor filtering, and combined filtering, evaluated as real-plus-synthetic augmentation (\rsr{}) rather than standalone synthetic substitution. We emphasize that \dacpf{} is a plausibility filter, not causal discovery or effect estimation: ``causal'' describes the clinical origin of the anchor hypotheses, not a claim that the filtering step identifies a causal effect.

\subsection{Blocked $2^2$ Factorial Experiment}
\label{sec:factorial}



The mechanism study uses a two-factor, two-level factorial design: separability weighting is factor $A$, clinical-anchor weighting is factor $B$, and all four combinations are evaluated on the same fold-specific candidate pool. Fold is included as a block because conditions within a fold are paired rather than independent replications.

Let $A \in \{-1,+1\}$ encode separability weighting and $B \in \{-1,+1\}$ anchor weighting. The fold-level response is the classifier-averaged \tstr{} Macro-F1,
\begin{equation}
y_{ijk} = \mu + q_A A_i + q_B B_j + q_{AB} A_i B_j + \gamma_k + \varepsilon_{ijk},
\label{eq:blocked}
\end{equation}



where $y_{ijk}$ is the classifier-averaged Macro-F1 for separability level $i$, anchor level $j$, and fold $k$; $\mu$ is the grand mean; $A_i$ and $B_j$ are the coded factor levels; $q_A$, $q_B$, and $q_{AB}$ are the separability, anchor, and interaction coefficients; $\gamma_k$ is the fixed effect of fold $k$; and $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is residual error. The high-minus-low factorial effects are $2q_A$, $2q_B$, and $2q_{AB}$. Type-II ANOVA partitions sums of squares among factors, interaction, fold, and residual error; the primary variation percentages exclude the fold block from the denominator so that factor contributions are compared against experimental error rather than against total variation. An unblocked $2^2r$ decomposition that treats folds as replications is also reported in the supplementary material for comparison, but Equation~\ref{eq:blocked} is the more defensible model for paired cross-validation conditions and is used for all primary conclusions.

Both raw and log-transformed responses are analysed. Residual normality is assessed using Shapiro--Wilk and Jarque--Bera tests \citep{shapiro1965,jarque1987}; constant variance is assessed with median-centred Levene (Brown--Forsythe), Fligner--Killeen, and Breusch--Pagan tests \citep{brown1974,fligner1976,breusch1979}. Durbin--Watson and lag-one Ljung--Box statistics diagnose ordering-related serial patterns but cannot establish independence \citep{durbin1950,ljung1978}. As noted in Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}, five-fold training sets overlap by 75\% and the four conditions within a fold are paired, so inference from this design is treated as exploratory rather than as a formal, independently replicated hypothesis test (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Common Four-task Augmentation and TSTR Experiments}
\label{sec:strengthened}

The augmentation experiment uses three repeated five-fold stratified partitions and compares real-only training, random oversampling, random undersampling, SMOTE, class-weighted training, standard CTGAN, \sepa{} CTGAN, standard TVAE, and \sepa{} TVAE. Synthetic conditions add positive candidates until the real training fold is balanced, the held-out fold retains its natural prevalence. All missing-value imputation is fitted on the real training fold before resampling or generation. The \sepa{} augmentation score uses realism plus separability with a fixed unit separability weight, no held-out outcome is used to tune the setting.

Logistic regression supplies a linear and calibration-sensitive reference, random forest supplies a nonlinear ensemble, and CatBoost supplies a strong tabular learner. Hyperparameters and the 0.5 classification threshold are held fixed across methods. Fold-level results are retained so generator comparisons can be paired within dataset, repeated partition, fold, and classifier. The manuscript uses three independently shuffled five-fold partitions with independent fold-specific generator seeds. These repetitions improve stability assessment but remain internal repeats of the same source samples rather than external validation.

The TSTR experiment uses the same repeated partitions. For each generator, standard and selected full synthetic training sets reproduce the real training-fold class counts and share one candidate pool. TSTR results are never pooled with augmentation results.

\subsection{Controlled Prevalence-by-overlap Experiment}
\label{sec:controlled}

A separate mechanism experiment varies class prevalence and overlap independently and is not counted as a third health dataset. We generated 2000-record, 15-feature binary-classification problems with 8 informative features, 3 redundant features, 2 clusters per class, and 2\% label noise. Prevalence was set to 5\%, 15\%, or 30\%, and the class separation to 0.4, 1.0, or 2.0, corresponding respectively to high, moderate, and low overlap. Each of the 9 cells used 10 independent data seeds and a stratified 70/30 train-test split.

The comparison includes real-only training, random oversampling, SMOTE, and a SepAware-SMOTE condition. For the latter, SMOTE generates five times the number of required positive-class candidates, the selector ranks them by nearest-real realism, nearest-hit/nearest-miss margin, and random-forest label confidence, then selects enough records to balance the training data. Logistic regression and random forest are evaluated with AUPRC, Macro-F1, and positive-class F1. A heteroscedasticity-robust linear model tests method-by-overlap and method-by-prevalence interactions.
