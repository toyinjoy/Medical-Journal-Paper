\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Synthetic tabular health data}

Statistical microdata synthesis, graphical models, and copulas generate records from an estimated joint distribution and remain useful when their assumptions are inspectable \citep{raghunathan2003,wang2021}. Generative adversarial networks instead learn through a discriminator--generator game \citep{goodfellow2014}. MedGAN adapted this idea to discrete patient records \citep{choi2017}, while CTGAN introduced mode-specific normalization, conditional vectors, and training-by-sampling for mixed-type tabular data \citep{xu2019}. Those properties make CTGAN a suitable fixed generator for the present question, which concerns curation within a generator rather than comparison between generator families.

Synthetic health data are commonly evaluated through resemblance, utility, and disclosure risk \citep{goncalves2020,hernandez2023,kaabachi2025}. Marginal and correlation measures assess selected distributional properties; origin classifiers test multivariate distinguishability; and train-on-synthetic, test-on-real (\tstr{}) evaluation asks whether predictive structure transfers to untouched real observations. No single measure establishes fitness for use. Aggregate fidelity can be dominated by the majority class, and strong downstream classification can result from a narrow set of easy synthetic prototypes rather than adequate representation of positive-class heterogeneity \citep{ghosheh2024,pezoulas2024}.

\subsection{Minority-class prediction and operating thresholds}

Imbalanced learning methods alter sample frequency, interpolation, decision costs, or operating thresholds \citep{chawla2002,he2009}. AUPRC is valuable for ranking under unequal prevalence, whereas minority-F1 evaluates classification at one operating point \citep{saito2015}. These endpoints answer different questions. In \tstr{}, the probability scale learned on synthetic records may not transfer to real records, so a fixed threshold of 0.5 can produce almost no positive predictions despite nontrivial ranking information. Threshold selection must therefore be contained within training data and evaluated jointly with calibration.

Candidate selection adds a distinct intervention. A generator defines what records are available; a selector determines which available modes and boundary regions are represented in the final training set. An oncology framework has similarly treated synthetic-dataset selection as a multi-dimensional decision rather than assuming that the first generated dataset is fit for purpose \citep{christoforou2025}. Our study differs by holding one candidate pool fixed within each comparison and intervening directly on its selected rows, which permits a cleaner separation of generation from curation.

\subsection{Study positioning}

The unresolved gap is not whether synthetic data can be scored after generation, but when transparent post-generation curation changes held-out-real minority utility and whether that change survives coverage and calibration checks. \sepa{} addresses this gap through a matched-pool design. Its contribution is an auditable selection operator and an empirical applicability envelope, not a claim that CTGAN or synthetic training is universally preferable to real-data modeling. Privacy is also outside the performance claim: synthetic data are not anonymous by construction, and nearest-record diagnostics cannot replace an explicit attack model or formal guarantee \citep{yale2019,dwork2006}.
