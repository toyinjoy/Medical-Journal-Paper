\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Four outcome tasks from three health-data sources were evaluated. This broadens the observed prevalence and outcome contexts but does not identify a general relationship between intrinsic overlap and SepAware benefit; the two Vigitel tasks share a source and are not independent cohorts.

The Vigitel factorial finding comes from one 5000-record sample, one cross-validation partition, one CTGAN configuration, and shared within-fold candidate pools. The common benchmark uses three repeated five-fold partitions and 30 training epochs. Fold-specific generator seeds avoid reusing a single fitted generator, and repeated shuffles measure partition variability, but all repetitions reuse the same source observations. More generator seeds, higher-capacity sensitivity runs, and independent datasets are necessary before confirmatory claims.

The fixed lambda grid was exploratory because performance and configuration selection used the same folds. The common analysis fixes the separability weight in advance, but it does not estimate a clinically optimal trade-off between discrimination and positive-class diversity. The score also depends on a random-forest confidence term; additional boundary models and label-permutation controls are needed to test classifier-specific shortcuts.

The TVAE condition is not directly symmetric with CTGAN. SDV 1.17 does not implement native TVAE conditional sampling, so TVAE was fitted to positive-class features. This is a reproducible conditional-generation strategy, but its low diversity shows that post-selection cannot compensate for a collapsed or highly concentrated candidate pool. A diffusion model and a generator with native conditional support should be added before making a broad generator-agnostic claim.

Positive-class coverage and diversity are geometric proxies rather than clinically validated phenotype measures. The COVID-19 death class may contain several pathways that are not represented by unsupervised distances. Blinded clinician review and prespecified phenotype or subgroup definitions are required to determine whether removed variation is clinically meaningful. Calibration estimates are also unstable in the small COVID-19 folds.

The privacy analysis is limited to exact matches, nearest-record distances, and a membership proxy. It does not include attribute inference, shadow-model membership attacks, linkage attacks, or a formal differential-privacy guarantee. No synthetic data release should be justified from the present privacy results.

Finally, external or temporal validation is absent. The smoking file contains no survey-year field, the obesity evaluation is not a prospective temporal split, and the COVID-19 cohort is institution-specific. Kidney mortality uses a longitudinal landmark but random patient partitions rather than a calendar-time split. At least one temporal or cross-site evaluation is needed.

These limitations define the next additions: a native conditional third generator, multiple generator seeds per fold, expert-informed positive-class coverage, stronger privacy attacks, formal overlap-to-benefit modeling, and external or temporal validation. Failures should continue to be reported as operational guidance about when separability control should not be used.
