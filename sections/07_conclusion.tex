\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Class imbalance and class overlap are not interchangeable problems. In the completed synthetic-to-real factorial experiment, explicit separability pressure explains the large Vigitel utility change but not the COVID-19 variation. In the strengthened augmentation experiment, SepAware improves CTGAN modestly on both datasets, does not improve TVAE, and is often matched or exceeded by random oversampling. Its closer minority coverage is accompanied by reduced diversity.

SepAware should therefore be understood as an auditable mechanism for controlling and studying class-conditional geometry, not as a universally superior augmentation method. Its practical use requires evidence that a predictive gain survives comparison with simple baselines and does not come from removing difficult but legitimate minority cases. The current two-dataset study establishes this conditional research direction and provides the experiment structure needed to add further datasets, seeds, generators, clinical coverage measures, and external validation.
