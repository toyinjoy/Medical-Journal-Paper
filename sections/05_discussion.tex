\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Principal finding}

The central finding is conditional rather than universal. With CTGAN, quotas, classifiers, and threshold policy held constant, Pareto curation improved locked minority-F1, Macro-F1, and AUPRC for the two Vigitel outcomes. It did not establish a primary benefit for COVID-19 or kidney mortality. The two positive tasks share a surveillance source and therefore define one observed operating regime rather than two independent external replications.

The Generate--Curate--Decide decomposition explains why this is more than a generator comparison. The shared pool determines what information is available. Curation alters how that information is represented, and the training-only threshold converts the resulting probability ranking into classifications. The progressive replacement experiment provides direct evidence that selected rows affect utility, while the epoch study shows that stronger generation can reduce the selection advantage. Neither generation quality nor curation alone determines the final result.

\subsection{Why curation helps}

The diagnostic pattern supports selection of locally plausible, decision-useful prototypes rather than general distributional improvement. Local precision increased while recall and rare-mode coverage decreased in every task. In the successful Vigitel regime, this concentration improved minority precision and recall at the learned threshold sufficiently to raise F1. It did not make every marginal distribution closer to real data and did not preserve the full positive-class support.

This distinction is important for synthetic health data. A curated training set can be useful for a discrimination task without being a faithful synthetic population for epidemiologic estimation, descriptive statistics, subgroup analysis, or release. The acceptable balance depends on intended use. If the objective is minority-case screening, a controlled reduction in redundant or noisy support may be tolerable. If the objective is calibrated risk, phenotype discovery, or population representation, the observed ECE and coverage losses may invalidate the method.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

COVID-19 combined 83 positive outcomes with 33 predictors. Its curation effects varied across generator seeds and classifiers, consistent with insufficient effective positive information for stable geometry and profile estimation. Stronger selection pressure cannot create information that the cohort does not contain. Dimensionality reduction or more events is preferable to further tuning on the same 334 records.

Kidney mortality combined 5.3\% prevalence, sparse zero-heavy event counts, and extensive repeated feature profiles. The shared candidate pool contained some underrepresented high-count trajectories, but the curator removed them disproportionately. Pure Pareto selection also failed the diversity floor, causing the final set to restore Standard rows heavily. A count-aware or longitudinal generator and a separate calibration strategy are more plausible next steps than stronger post-generation selection.

Generator budget is a third condition. On both Vigitel tasks, Standard CTGAN improved with longer training and sometimes narrowed or removed the curation advantage. Curation is therefore partly an information-recovery and efficiency mechanism for finite-budget pools. A compute-matched study would be required before claiming computational efficiency; the present results only establish training-budget sensitivity.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}

The competitiveness of simple resampling in health data has led to warnings that sophisticated synthesis may add sample count without adding information \citep{huetdastarac2026}. Our result refines that argument: even within one generator fit, downstream utility can depend materially on which candidates are retained. At the same time, the loss of support agrees with broader recommendations to evaluate synthetic data through multiple dimensions rather than a single global score \citep{hernandez2025,nanevski2026}.

CTGAN was fixed based on earlier development evidence rather than selected from the locked outcomes. This paper therefore makes no cross-generator claim. Its novelty lies in matched-pool curation, decision-transfer control, and failure analysis. This positioning also avoids interpreting a small architectural modification as the contribution; the contribution is a testable intervention over a fixed pool and an empirical account of when its utility does and does not survive safeguards.

\subsection{Implications}

For a data custodian, the proposed workflow is feasible only inside a trusted environment: real records fit CTGAN, define prototype geometry and profile compatibility, and support candidate scoring. An external analyst receives only the curated synthetic set and the documented modeling policy. Because privacy was not established, the present study does not recommend releasing those records. For a model developer, the primary practical lesson is to diagnose three failures separately: absence of useful modes at generation, destructive concentration during curation, and probability shift at decision transfer.

The locked results support one narrow claim: threshold-aware Pareto curation can improve synthetic-only minority classification in the observed Vigitel regime. They do not establish external transportability, patient benefit, calibrated risk prediction, or a privacy guarantee.
