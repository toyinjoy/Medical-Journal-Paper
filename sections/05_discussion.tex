\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation of the common benchmark}

SepAware selection produced a corrected AUPRC gain over standard CTGAN for Vigitel smoking. The other three CTGAN intervals and all four TVAE intervals included zero after accounting for repeated-cross-validation dependence, and the selected generator did not exceed the strongest conventional method on any task. The result is narrower than a claim that separability-aware generation improves rare-outcome prediction: selection can improve a specific candidate pool, but it did not displace real-only training or resampling as the practical reference.

The distinction between TSTR and augmentation is material. TSTR asks whether a synthetic dataset retains enough information to replace real training data. Augmentation asks whether synthetic positive records add information when the real observations remain available. A method can improve substitution utility relative to its generator while adding little in augmentation. We therefore report the regimes separately and avoid averaging across them.

The two Vigitel outcomes share a source. Agreement between their estimates may reflect common measurement, preprocessing, or population structure and cannot be interpreted as replication in an independent cohort. COVID-19 mortality has a small sample and broad uncertainty; kidney mortality has lower prevalence and a longitudinally derived feature set. This heterogeneity is useful for observing failure, but four outcome tasks do not establish transportability.

\subsection{Selection, coverage, and diversity}

CTGAN selection moved synthetic positive records closer to held-out positive observations in every task. At the same time, pairwise diversity decreased in every task. These changes are compatible with two explanations: the selector may retain clearer class-conditional signal, or it may discard legitimate boundary cases. The available labels cannot determine which removed variation is measurement noise, redundant spread, or clinically meaningful heterogeneity.

The kidney task illustrates the limitation. Coverage distance improved and diversity declined, yet its corrected CTGAN AUPRC interval included zero and real-only training retained the highest mean AUPRC. Geometry changed as designed without producing a demonstrated ranking benefit. A useful deployment criterion must therefore include predictive performance against simple baselines, calibration, and clinically interpretable coverage rather than the composite selection score alone.

TVAE also shows dependence on candidate-pool quality. Under SDV 1.17.2, class-specific models were required because native outcome-conditional sampling was unavailable. Their positive-class diversity was low, and subsequent selection did not create information absent from the pool. The scoring rule can be applied to different generators, but its benefit is not generator independent.

\subsection{Dataset-specific mechanism evidence}

The Vigitel-obesity factorial estimate attributes a Macro-F1 change to separability weighting within that executed design; the corresponding COVID-19 interval includes zero. The factorial results reuse overlapping folds and shared candidate pools, so their small-sample intervals are exploratory. The anchor component is also task-specific. COVID-19 anchors were defined from available clinical variables, whereas defensible analogues were not prespecified for smoking or kidney mortality. Extending those anchors by analogy after observing outcomes would weaken, not strengthen, the comparison.

The controlled simulation separates overlap from prevalence but does not show a significant method-by-overlap interaction. It supports the design premise that imbalance and overlap are different properties; it does not establish a threshold at which SepAware should be used.

\subsection{Calibration and operating thresholds}

The kidney results show that a model can have useful ranking while predicting no positives at a fixed 0.5 threshold. Undersampling increased recall but substantially worsened the Brier score. Threshold selection should therefore depend on the intended clinical operating point and asymmetric error costs. This study fixed the threshold and did not tune it on test folds; it does not recommend a deployment threshold.

\subsection{Privacy interpretation}

Augmentation exact-match rates were low, but standalone kidney TVAE data had exact-match rates of 10.5\% under standard sampling and 13.4\% after selection. Several real-versus-synthetic detection AUROCs were also close to one. These findings argue against releasing those synthetic data. Membership-proxy AUROCs near 0.5 do not offset that concern: the proxy attacker uses nearest-synthetic distance and does not represent stronger shadow-model, linkage, or attribute-inference attacks. Disclosure risk must be assessed under an explicit attacker model before any synthetic records are released.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}

The competitiveness of random oversampling agrees with evidence that sampling with replacement can match more complex augmentation in small-data settings \citep{huetdastarac2026}. Our results add a within-generator comparison: selecting candidates can change utility and geometry without making synthetic augmentation the best strategy. Evaluation frameworks that separate fidelity, utility, and privacy are therefore more informative than a single global quality score \citep{hernandez2025,nanevski2026}.

The study supports treating separability as a measurable intervention rather than an assumed virtue. Its practical value depends on the task, generator, candidate pool, classifier, and acceptable loss of positive-class heterogeneity. External validation and phenotype-aware coverage analyses are needed before the method can support a clinical claim.
