\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Synthetic health data may support model development when real records cannot leave a trusted environment, but a generator can produce a large candidate pool without preserving the class-conditional structure required for minority-case detection. Evaluation commonly treats generation as the final step and does not isolate the effect of curating which synthetic records are retained.

\textbf{Objective:} We tested when separability-aware post-generation curation improves train-on-synthetic, test-on-real minority classification relative to matched standard sampling, and whether gains are accompanied by losses in coverage or calibration.

\textbf{Methods:} We organized the study as Generate--Curate--Decide. A fixed conditional tabular generative adversarial network (CTGAN) produced a five-times class-conditional candidate pool. \sepa{} curated records using epsilon-Pareto ordering of class-conditional realism, cross-fitted separability, and prespecified profile compatibility, followed by a diversity repair requiring at least 80\% of matched standard-sampling diversity. Logistic regression, random forest, and CatBoost learned operating thresholds from grouped out-of-fold predictions on synthetic training data only. Four outcome tasks were evaluated: Vigitel obesity, COVID-19 hospital mortality, Vigitel current smoking, and kidney-cohort 365-day mortality; the Vigitel tasks share one source. The candidate, endpoint, and inference plan were frozen before the internal locked outcomes were accessed. Minority-class F1 was primary; Macro-F1, AUPRC, calibration, coverage, and diversity were secondary.

\textbf{Results:} Locked minority-F1 differences were 0.006 for COVID-19 (95\% CI $-0.121$ to 0.131; Holm $p=.925$), 0.056 for kidney mortality (0.013 to 0.107; $p=.057$), 0.112 for Vigitel obesity (0.074 to 0.150; $p<.001$), and 0.143 for Vigitel smoking (0.096 to 0.190; $p<.001$). Both Vigitel effects won all 9 generator-seed--classifier pairs and were accompanied by Macro-F1 and AUPRC gains. Expected calibration error increased by 0.099 and 0.085, respectively. In saved-pool diagnostics, curation increased local minority precision but reduced nearest-neighbor recall and rare-mode coverage in all four tasks. Progressive replacement of standard-selected rows with \sepa{} rows changed held-out utility without regenerating the pool, supporting a selection effect while identifying COVID-19 and kidney mortality as boundary conditions.

\textbf{Conclusions:} Post-generation curation can recover decision-useful minority prototypes when the candidate pool contains adequate class-conditional structure, but it is not a universal improvement and does not produce calibrated risk estimates. Utility must be interpreted jointly with support coverage, diversity, and training-budget sensitivity. This internal locked study does not establish external transportability, clinical benefit, or privacy.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} synthetic health data; tabular data; minority-class prediction; candidate selection; CTGAN; train on synthetic test on real; calibration
