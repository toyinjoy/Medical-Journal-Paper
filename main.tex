\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Synthetic data are used to increase positive-class sample size in rare-outcome prediction, but additional records may not improve prediction when classes overlap or generation fails to preserve class-conditional structure.

\textbf{Objective:} We tested whether post-generation control of class separability improves held-out-real utility beyond class-frequency correction and whether any gain is accompanied by reduced positive-class diversity.

\textbf{Methods:} \sepa{} ranks an oversized synthetic candidate pool using nearest-real proximity, nearest-hit/nearest-miss margin, and probability from a real-trained classifier while preserving prespecified class counts. We evaluated Vigitel obesity, COVID-19 hospital mortality, Vigitel current smoking, and kidney-cohort 365-day mortality; the two Vigitel tasks share one source. Three repeated 5-fold partitions compared real-only training, random over- and undersampling, SMOTE, class weighting, CTGAN, TVAE, and their selected variants. AUPRC was the primary augmentation endpoint. TSTR, calibration, fidelity, positive-class coverage and diversity, exact matches, and a membership proxy were reported separately. Primary paired tests used a corrected repeated-cross-validation procedure and Holm adjustment.

\textbf{Results:} Mean AUPRC differences between \sepa{} and standard CTGAN were 0.012 for COVID-19, 0.009 for Vigitel obesity, 0.031 for Vigitel smoking, and 0.005 for kidney mortality. Only smoking excluded zero after repeated-cross-validation correction and Holm adjustment (95\% CI 0.015--0.048; adjusted $p=.009$). All four TVAE intervals included zero. Selected CTGAN did not exceed the strongest conventional comparator on any task. CTGAN selection reduced positive-class coverage distance in every task but also reduced diversity. Exact-match rates were at most 0.0005 and membership-proxy AUROCs were near 0.5; these are not privacy guarantees.

\textbf{Conclusions:} Class-boundary control differs from class balancing, but the evidence supports a generator-relative augmentation gain only for the smoking task. Selection should be compared with simple baselines and assessed for calibration and loss of positive-class heterogeneity. Internal cross-validation does not establish clinical benefit, transportability, or privacy.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} synthetic health data; tabular data; class imbalance; class overlap; rare outcomes; data augmentation; privacy; calibration
